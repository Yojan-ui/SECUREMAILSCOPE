Risk narrative

rule-based writer

broken.example scores 22/100 (F) — high risk. The assessment found 3 high-severity and 7 lower-severity issues. The most consequential — DMARC policy is p=none (monitoring only) — matters because: p=none asks receivers to report on failures but to deliver the mail anyway. A message forged as @broken.example that fails both SPF and DKIM still lands in the recipient's inbox, so the domain remains usable for phishing and invoice-fraud campaigns. Monitoring mode is the correct first step, but it is not enforcement. The practical consequence is that broken.example is currently spoofable: an attacker can put your domain in the From: header of a phishing or invoice-fraud message and receiving servers have no instruction to stop it.

What an attacker could do with this

  • Forged mail is reported to you but still delivered. A spoofing campaign runs to completion; you find out afterwards from the aggregate reports, which is useful for forensics and useless for prevention.
  • Unlisted senders get a neutral result, which receivers weigh the same as having no policy at all.
  • Any host inside the authorised range passes SPF for your domain. On shared hosting or a cloud provider's range, that includes machines you do not control.
  • Nobody receives the daily reports naming the hosts that send as your domain, so an ongoing spoofing campaign produces no signal your team can act on.
  • Receivers are told to treat signature failures as if the message were unsigned, so the signature provides no enforcement benefit.

RFC 8301 §3.2

medium

MTA-STS is not configured

MTA-STS

No MTA-STS record exists at _mta-sts.broken.example. SMTP's opportunistic TLS can be stripped by an on-path attacker simply by removing the STARTTLS advertisement from the server greeting — the sending server then delivers the message in cleartext and nothing alerts either party. MTA-STS is the mechanism that makes TLS mandatory and detectable for inbound mail.

Fix  Publish TXT "v=STSv1; id=<timestamp>" at _mta-sts.broken.example and host a policy file at https://mta-sts.broken.example/.well-known/mta-sts.txt, starting in mode: testing before moving to mode: enforce.
