Risk narrative

rule-based writer

spoofable.example scores 24/100 (F) — high risk. The assessment found 2 critical and 3 lower-severity issues. The most consequential — No DMARC record published — matters because: No DMARC policy exists at _dmarc.spoofable.example. Nothing instructs receiving servers what to do with mail that fails SPF or DKIM, and no one receives reports about who is sending as this domain. In practice this means the domain can be spoofed in phishing and business-email-compromise campaigns with no enforcement standing in the way. The practical consequence is that spoofable.example is currently spoofable: an attacker can put your domain in the From: header of a phishing or invoice-fraud message and receiving servers have no instruction to stop it.

What an attacker could do with this

  • An attacker can send mail with your exact domain in the From: header and no receiving server has instructions to reject it. The usual play is an invoice or payment-detail change sent to your finance team or your customers, arriving from what looks precisely like your domain.
  • The record explicitly authorises every host on the internet to send as your domain. An attacker's mail does not merely evade SPF — it passes it.
  • If no DKIM signature is applied, forwarded mail loses SPF alignment and DMARC has only one signal left to work with, which increases both spoofing exposure and false rejections of your own legitimate mail.
  • Without MTA-STS, an on-path attacker strips the STARTTLS advertisement from your server's greeting. The sending server sees a host that does not support TLS, falls back to cleartext, and delivers the message in the clear. Neither side sees an error.
  • No one receives reports of failed TLS negotiations, so a downgrade attack or an expired certificate on your MX produces no alert to your team.

SPF

RFC 6376 §3.6

medium

MTA-STS is not configured

MTA-STS

No MTA-STS record exists at _mta-sts.spoofable.example. SMTP's opportunistic TLS can be stripped by an on-path attacker simply by removing the STARTTLS advertisement from the server greeting — the sending server then delivers the message in cleartext and nothing alerts either party. MTA-STS is the mechanism that makes TLS mandatory and detectable for inbound mail.

Fix  Publish TXT "v=STSv1; id=<timestamp>" at _mta-sts.spoofable.example and host a policy file at https://mta-sts.spoofable.example/.well-known/mta-sts.txt, starting in mode: testing before moving to mode: enforce.
